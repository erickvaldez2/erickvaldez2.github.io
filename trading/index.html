      The basics

      Learn these before any method. Position size decides whether you survive long enough to get good.

      R
      The amount you risk on one trade. With $1,000 and 1% risk, 1R = $10. A trade that makes $20 is +2R; one that hits the stop is −1R. (Van Tharp)
      Stop-loss
      The price where you admit you're wrong and get out. Decide it before you enter, never move it further away.
      Position size
      How many shares or coins to buy so that hitting the stop costs exactly 1R. Size = risk $ ÷ (entry − stop).
      Reward : risk
      Distance to target ÷ distance to stop. At 2:1 or better, you can be wrong more than half the time and still make money.
      VWAP
      Volume-weighted average price for the day. Above it, buyers are in control; below it, sellers are.
      50 / 200-day MA
      Average closing price over 50 or 200 days. Rising and price above them = healthy long-term trend.
      Support / resistance
      Prices where buyers (support) or sellers (resistance) stepped in before. Good places for entries and stops.
      Worked example ($1,000 account, 1% risk)
      Risk budget = $1,000 × 1% = $10 Entry $50.00, stop $49.50 → risk per share $0.50 Size = $10 ÷ $0.50 = 20 shares ($1,000 position) Target $51.00 → reward $1.00 per share → 2:1 Win: +$20 (+2R) Loss: −$10 (−1R)
